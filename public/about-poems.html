About Primary Care POEMs

POEMs (Patient-Oriented Evidence that Matters) are short summaries of new research written for primary care clinicians. Thousands of research articles are published every month, and only a small fraction of them should change how primary care is practiced. POEMs find those studies, judge how trustworthy they are, and say in plain language what they mean for patients.

How studies are selected

Every month, a team of family physicians with training in evidence-based medicine (the POETs) screens more than 80 medical journals. A study becomes a POEM only if it meets three criteria:

  • It addresses a question primary care clinicians face. The problem is common, or important, in everyday practice.
  • It measures outcomes that patients care about. These are symptoms, function, quality of life, illness and death, rather than laboratory values or other intermediate measures that may not translate into patient benefit.
  • It has the potential to change practice. If its results are valid, clinicians would need to do something differently: start, stop or change a test or treatment.

The study must also be valid: well designed and well conducted, with results unlikely to be explained by bias or chance. Only a small percentage of the articles screened meet all of these criteria.

How POEMs are written

Each POEM follows the same structure:

  • Clinical question: the question the study answers, in one sentence.
  • Bottom line: the answer, and what it means for practice.
  • Reference: the original article, with a link to PubMed.
  • Study design, funding, allocation and setting: how the study was done and where.
  • Synopsis: a description of the study, its patients and its results, with a critical appraisal of its strengths and weaknesses.

Each POEM is assigned a level of evidence, from 1a (the strongest, such as a high-quality systematic review of randomized trials) to 5 (the weakest, such as expert opinion), so readers can see at a glance how much confidence the result deserves. Results are reported in terms clinicians can use, such as absolute differences and the number needed to treat.

How POEMs are reviewed

Each draft POEM is peer-reviewed by members of the University of Missouri Department of Family Medicine, updated by the POETs, and copy-edited. Reviewers check the selection criteria, the appraisal of the study's validity, the accuracy of the numbers against the original article, and whether the bottom line follows from the evidence.

Independence

POEMs are written independently of the study authors, journals and manufacturers. Each POEM reports how the study was funded, so readers can take this into account.
