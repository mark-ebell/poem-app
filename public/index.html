      Upload a research article (PDF)

      Upload a research study (PDF) and press the button to generate the first draft of a POEM (Patient Oriented Evidence that Matters). The text of the article is sent to Claude, an AI assistant, which writes the draft; this can take up to a minute. The draft then opens in the Edit POEMs tab, where you can review and edit it, choose the POET and publication date, and save it to the repository. PDFs that are scanned images without a text layer cannot be read.

      Administrator: import a monthly batch of POEMs from Word

      Choose the month's Word file and the month it will be published. Each POEM should start with a "Title:" line. Nothing is saved until you review the list that appears and click Import. Missing PubMed IDs are looked up at PubMed.

      Instructions on searching

      Use AND, OR and NOT (in capitals) to combine words, and brackets to group them. Put an exact phrase in quotes. Words with no operator between them are combined with AND. Use * for the rest of a word (hypertens* finds hypertension and hypertensive). Example: "atrial fibrillation" AND dabigatran NOT warfarin.

      POEMs: searches the title, reference, clinical question, synopsis, bottom line, POEM number and PubMed ID; the time span, age group and category apply to POEMs only. Chapters: the full text. History and Physical and Diagnostic Calculators: the finding or test, the symptom, the disease and the specialty. Risk Scores: the name and description.

      Evidence Summary of POEMs, Cochrane library, systematic reviews and guidelines

      Use AND, OR and NOT (in capitals) to combine words, brackets to group them, quotes for an exact phrase, and * for the rest of a word, as on the Search POEMs tab. Example: "atrial fibrillation" AND dabigatran

      Downloads a Word document that begins with the matching POEMs, grouped by category, followed by articles from PubMed organised by content area.

              Differential Diagnosis

              Search or select a presenting sign, symptom, or test abnormality to see its differential diagnosis, ranked from most to least common.

                Select an item above to see its ranked differential diagnosis.

                  Very common Common Less common Uncommon Rare ⚑ Don't miss

                  About this tool. Rankings are qualitative clinical estimates of relative frequency among patients presenting with each finding in general adult primary care / ED settings — they are not derived from a specific cited study, and true frequency varies by age, setting, and population. The "Don't miss" flag marks diagnoses that are dangerous enough to always consider even when they are not the most likely cause; it is independent of the frequency ranking. This tool is for educational reference only and is not a substitute for clinical judgment.
